Rovie is prepaid. You put credit on an account, calls draw it down, and when it
reaches zero the calls stop. No card on file, no subscription, no invoice at
the end of the month, and nothing that can overdraft.

That is not a simplification for its own sake. A dollar-denominated card
subscription is exactly the thing that stops most of this continent's
developers from buying tokens at all.

## The two currencies

Rovie holds your balance in **USD**, because that is what the providers charge
in. You fund it in **your own currency**, and you are quoted in your own
currency throughout.

<Steps>

1. **You pay** ₦25,000 by bank transfer.
2. **Rovie converts** at the rate locked at that moment, minus its markup.
3. **Your balance** goes up by the resulting USD figure.
4. **Calls draw down** that USD balance at the providers' per-token rates.
5. **The dashboard shows** the remainder in both — USD, and naira at today's
   rate.

</Steps>

The rate is locked when the charge is created, not when the money lands. A
quote you were shown before starting a payment is marked *indicative* for
exactly that reason: rates move, and the binding one is the one on the charge.

## Paying

Two methods, from [Billing](https://rovie.africa/dashboard/billing):

**Bank transfer** — the accessible option, and the default. Most people have a
bank account and no stablecoin. Settled in NGN, UGX, GHS or KES.

**Stablecoin** — for anyone already holding one. Carries a hard provider
minimum of roughly $1.45, which bank transfer does not.

Minimums differ by method, and the dashboard shows the one that applies to what
you selected — expressed in your own currency, in minor units, converted
server-side. Where a currency's rate is briefly unavailable, it falls back to
showing USD rather than printing a wrong local figure.

<Aside type="note" title="Quoting and paying are different lists">
Rovie can quote a price in every African currency its rate feed carries — that
is what the map and the models page use. It can *settle* a payment in a shorter
list: **NGN, UGX, GHS, KES**. Holding funds in a currency is a compliance
question, not a technical one, so that list grows slowly and deliberately.
</Aside>

## The markup

Rovie charges the provider's own per-token rate plus a percentage. That
percentage is shown on the quote before you pay, and it is the entire business
model — there is no subscription, no per-request fee, and no charge for a
request that failed before reaching a provider.

What you are buying with it: the dollar relationship with nine providers, the
FX, and the payment rails. Doing that yourself requires a dollar card with a
limit high enough to matter.

## When a payment goes quiet

Payments confirm by webhook from the provider. Usually it lands in seconds.

If a transfer shows as **Waiting for payment** after you have definitely paid,
open the payment on the
[billing page](https://rovie.africa/dashboard/billing) and refresh it — that
asks the provider directly what happened rather than waiting for a webhook that
may not arrive. It is the same check that runs automatically, on demand.

Still stuck after that: [hello@rovie.africa](mailto:hello@rovie.africa) with
the payment reference. Money that left your account and did not arrive is a
support case, not something to keep retrying.

## Running out

When the balance hits zero, every key on the account returns
[`402`](/guides/errors/#402--out-of-balance). Nothing queues, nothing degrades,
nothing is charged — the call simply does not happen.

Set the **low-balance email** on
[your account settings](https://rovie.africa/dashboard/account): a USD figure
below which Rovie emails you. Pick a number that covers a few days of your
normal burn, so a top-up is something you schedule rather than something you do
at 2am.

## Watching the spend

- [Usage](https://rovie.africa/dashboard/usage) — where the money went, by
  model, provider and day. Also a CSV export.
- [Per-key budgets](/guides/limits/#budgets) — a hard lifetime ceiling per key,
  so one runaway job cannot spend the balance.
- [`GET /models`](/api/operations/listcatalog/) — the per-million rates, if you
  want to estimate before running something large.

[Usage and cost tracking](/guides/usage/) covers the reconciliation in detail.

## Refunds and closing the account

Unspent credit is exactly that — unspent. If you delete your account you
forfeit whatever is left, which is why the deletion flow makes you type the
figure you are giving up rather than accepting a generic confirmation. An
anonymised record is kept afterwards where the payment ledger needs it; see the
[privacy policy](https://rovie.africa/privacy).

For anything else — a payment that credited the wrong amount, a top-up you want
reversed — email [hello@rovie.africa](mailto:hello@rovie.africa).
